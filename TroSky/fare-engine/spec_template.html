Technical specification · reference implementation

Fare, driver payout and platform commission engine for Ghana

The engine does three things for every trip: it prices the rider, pays the driver, and works out what the platform keeps. It follows the structure ride-hailing platforms publicly describe: a metered fare multiplied by a demand multiplier, then a percentage service fee. It is not any company’s internal code.

Summary

The required rate is GH¢7.10 per 8 km Given. The engine stores those two numbers and derives the per-km rate on every use: 7.10 ÷ 8 = GH¢0.8875 per km, exact in Decimal. Every other number lives in one configuration file and can change without a code release.

DecisionChoice in the referenceWhy
ArithmeticPython Decimal; floats refused at every entry point, including the config file7.10 has no exact binary float. Float errors break ROUND_HALF_UP at exact halves such as 3.55.
Default pricingMode A, linear distanceFare grows smoothly with distance. Per-block pricing has price cliffs (8.0 km costs GH¢7.10, 8.1 km costs GH¢14.20).
RoundingFare to the nearest GH¢0.10, platform fee to GH¢0.01, both ROUND_HALF_UP VerifyCoins and MoMo amounts riders recognise. The driver gets fare minus the rounded fee, so the two always sum to the fare exactly.
SurgeStatic factor table now, live demand and supply later, capped at 2.0 with a kill switchThe table is a prior until there is data. The cap, smoothing and kill switch survive every later model.
QuotesUpfront and locked for 5 minutes AssumptionA surge change after the rider confirms never changes their price.
AccountingDouble-entry ledger, append-only, idempotency key on every postingEvery pesewa is traceable. Retries cannot double-post, and corrections are visible reversals.
Cash tripsThe fee becomes a receivable from the driver, netted against digital earnings or paid by MoMoThe driver already holds the fare, so the platform must collect its share afterwards.

Formulas

d is distance in km, t is minutes, m is the surge multiplier. Rounding happens once for the fare and once for the fee, never in between.

QuantityFormulaNotes
Per-km ratefare_reference ÷ reference_distance_km = 7.10 ÷ 8 = 0.8875Derived, never stored rounded. The engine multiplies before it divides, so a rate like 7.10 ÷ 3 is never cut short.
Mode A, linearraw = (base_fare + fare_reference × d ÷ reference_distance_km + per_minute_rate × t) × mBase and per-minute default to GH¢0.00.
Mode A, per blockdistance term = fare_reference × ⌈d ÷ reference_distance_km⌉Every started 8 km block costs GH¢7.10. Zero km is zero blocks, so the minimum fare applies.
Mode B, bandsraw = band_fare(d) × mFirst band with d ≤ its upper limit; band edges belong to the cheaper band. Beyond 40 km the quote is refused.
Farefare = round(max(minimum_fare, raw), 0.10, HALF_UP)The config loader refuses a minimum fare that is not on the rounding grid.
Surge, staticm = min(cap, max(1.0, 1 + Σ active uplifts)), stepped to 0.01Cap 2.0. The multiplier never discounts.
Surge, livetarget = f(demand ÷ supply); m′ = m + α × (target − m), with |m′ − m| ≤ max_stepf is straight lines between configured points. Snaps to the target within one step. No drivers counts as one.
Platform feefee = round(fare × service_fee_rate, 0.01, HALF_UP)On the fare including surge. Tolls and tips are excluded.
Driver payoutpayout = fare − fee + tolls + tips + incentivesPromotions never reduce it.
Rider paysrider_pays = fare − promo + tolls + tipsPromotions are platform-funded.
Platform netnet = fee − promo − incentivesCan be negative on a promoted trip.
Invariantrider_pays + promo + incentives = payout + feeChecked on every settlement and by the database.

Mode A vs Mode B

Mode A charges the same rate for every kilometre. Mode B copies the tapered shared-taxi schedule Verify against the official GPRTU/GRTCC schedule. Both at surge 1.0:

{{comparison}}

Mode A is above the tapered schedule for every distance beyond 1 km. At 8 km it charges GH¢7.10 against GH¢4.70. Per-block pricing is higher still on short trips: 1 km costs a full GH¢7.10 block.

Trade-offs

  • Driver economics favour Mode A. At a 20% fee the driver keeps 0.8875 × 0.80 = GH¢0.71 per km, before fuel and vehicle costs. At 25% it is GH¢0.67. Whether that covers costs depends on fuel prices per km Verify.
  • Platform revenue scales with the fare, so Mode A earns more per trip at the same fee rate.
  • Rider competitiveness favours Mode B. A rider who compares against trotro sees Mode A between 24% and 91% higher across these distances. Trotro is a shared minibus, so a private car usually costs more to run per passenger.
  • Middle paths to test in Phase 5: linear up to a distance, then tapered; a shared-ride product at Mode B prices; or a lower per-km rate. Each is a configuration change.

Surge

Static factor table, a prior

multiplier = min(2.0, max(1.0, 1 + sum of active uplifts))

Every uplift is an Assumption to calibrate with real demand data, and each carries its own start and end date in the config. Time windows include their start and exclude their end. Late night wraps past midnight. Storm counts as heavy rain. An event counts only when the pickup is within its radius.

The prompt does not name the four stacked factors. The reference uses evening rush, heavy rain, festive season and a major event: 0.30 + 0.40 + 0.25 + 0.30 = 1.25 of uplift, which reproduces the prompt’s uncapped 2.25.

Live surge in Phase 3, forecasting in Phase 4

  • Zones. The city is divided into H3 hexagons. Each zone counts open requests and free drivers every 120 seconds Assumption.
  • Target. The demand-to-supply ratio maps to a target multiplier along straight lines between configured points: 1.0→1.0, 1.5→1.2, 2.0→1.5, 3.0→2.0 Assumption.
  • Smoothing. Each interval closes 30% of the gap and moves at most 0.10, so prices drift instead of jumping.
  • Cap and kill switch. The same 2.0 cap applies. The kill switch forces 1.0 everywhere, for emergencies or a regulator’s request.
  • Forecasting. In Phase 4, time of day, weather and events become inputs to a demand forecast per zone, instead of separate uplift rules. The forecast moves drivers before the rain starts, and the live ratio sets the price.

What the rider sees

A raised price always carries a plain reason built from the active factors, such as “High demand: evening rush, rain”. The quote is then locked: once the rider confirms, the price cannot change, whatever happens to surge.

Fees and payouts

  1. Upfront quote. Price the predicted distance and time with the pickup zone’s multiplier, apply the minimum, round, and lock it for 5 minutes.
  2. Platform fee. fare × service_fee_rate, to the pesewa, on the fare including surge. Tolls and tips are excluded.
  3. Driver payout. fare − fee + tolls + tips + incentives.
  4. Promotions come out of the platform’s share and never out of the driver’s.
  5. Cash trips. The driver keeps the cash. The fee becomes a receivable, netted against digital earnings at the next payout or paid by MoMo.
  6. Digital trips. The platform collects through MoMo or card, keeps the fee, and pays the driver weekly Assumption. Balances under GH¢10.00 roll over Placeholder.

Edge cases

CaseRule in the reference
Zero distancePriced at the minimum fare. A completed trip with 0 km should be flagged for review before charging Assumption.
Below the minimumThe minimum applies to the surged amount, so surge cannot lift a short trip above GH¢1.90 until the raw fare passes it.
GPS errorPoints implying more than 150 km/h are dropped. GPS distance is capped at the routed distance plus 25%. If GPS is lost, the routed distance is used.
Trip changes after the quoteWithin 15% of the predicted distance, or within 0.5 km for short trips, the rider pays the locked quote. Beyond that, or when the rider changes the route, the trip is repriced at the locked multiplier.
Surge changes after the quoteIgnored. The locked multiplier is used even when the trip is repriced.
Quote expiryA quote confirmed after 5 minutes is refused, and the rider gets a fresh one.
Cancelled before pickupThe configured cancellation fee, GH¢0.00 for now Placeholder.
Cancelled mid-tripDistance travelled at the locked multiplier, with the minimum fare, never more than the quote.
Rider disputeSupport reviews the GPS trace and the receipt. The outcome is a full or partial refund.
RefundsAbsorbed by the platform, or, when a dispute rules against the driver, recovered from the driver’s next payout as a receivable.
Fully discounted tripNo MoMo request is sent; the engine refuses a zero collection.
Distance out of rangeOver 200 km in Mode A, or over 40 km in Mode B, is refused and handled by hand.
Payout failsThe money returns to the driver’s balance and goes out in the next run.
Late MoMo approvalA payment that timed out on our side but succeeded at MTN appears in reconciliation. It is honoured if the trip is unpaid, otherwise refunded.
Retried callbacks and requestsEvery posting has an idempotency key. A repeat returns the first result, and a repeat with different amounts is refused.

The PostgreSQL schema in schema.sql enforces these rules in the database. A constraint trigger checks at commit that each transaction balances. Triggers refuse any update or delete of ledger rows, and a unique index on the idempotency key stops double posting. The schema was loaded into a throwaway PostgreSQL 17 database, which was then removed. In that check a balanced entry committed, and an unbalanced entry, an edit, a reused key and a mismatched account type were all refused.

API contract

JSON over HTTPS. Money is a string with two decimals, such as "7.10", so no client parses it as a float. Every POST requires an Idempotency-Key header.

Daily reconciliation

  1. Gather. At 02:00 Accra time, take yesterday’s payment attempts, payouts and completed digital trips, plus MTN’s transaction records for the same day Verify how MTN provides them.
  2. Match on the reference sent with each request.
  3. Sort every exception into one of five buckets: missing at MoMo, unexpected at MoMo (often a late approval), amount mismatch, duplicate charge, or completed trip without payment.
  4. Act. Honour or refund late approvals, refund duplicates, and chase unpaid trips. Nothing posts to the ledger without a person approving it.
  5. Measure. Mismatch rate is exceptions divided by attempts checked. Alert above [0.1]% Placeholder.

The reference reconcile() function implements the matching and the five buckets, and a unit test covers each one.

Open questions

  1. Service fee rate: 20%, 25% or another figure? Confirm the current rate before launch Verify.
  2. GH¢7.10 per 8 km: linear or per-block? The reference defaults to linear.
  3. Rounding: is nearest GH¢0.10 with halves rounding up the rule? Should the fee also round to GH¢0.10?
  4. Minimum fare: is GH¢1.90 right for a private car, given it stays in force up to about 2.2 km?
  5. Tax: does VAT apply to the platform fee, and is it inside the fee or added to it? How do GRA vehicle income tax and DVLA fees apply to drivers? Verify, do not assume
  6. Trotro schedule: where is the official GPRTU/GRTCC schedule published, and how often does it change?
  7. MTN costs: what are the collection and disbursement fees, and who bears them? Verify
  8. Cash arrears: above what amount owed should a driver stop taking cash trips?
  9. Payouts: weekly or daily, and what minimum payout?
  10. Surge cap: is 2.0 acceptable to riders and regulators?
  11. Cancellation fee: how much, and after how long?

Reference code

The code lives in the TroSky repository under TroSky/fare-engine. It is standard library only, needs Python 3.11 or newer for the TOML reader, and was tested on Python 3.14. A CI workflow also runs the tests on Python 3.11 and 3.12. The backend and apps do not use the engine yet.
